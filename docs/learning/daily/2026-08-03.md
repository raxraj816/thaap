## 2. Payment Processing Concepts Learned

- A payment's history is a *stream*, and stream version is the only defensible
  ordering of it. Business timestamps are displayed evidence, never identity.
  Two events recorded in the same microsecond must still have an unambiguous
  order, and if the browser re-sorts by a rendered timestamp, an analyst can
  read a fraud decision as happening before the attempt that caused it.
- Three confirmations on a review-held payment produce only two completed
  attempts: the third is held by the REVIEW decision and never completes. This
  is not obvious from the fixture shape, which is exactly why the timeline test
  reconciles its count against `domain_events` instead of hard-coding two.
- `fraud_decisions` carries `payment_attempt_id`, not `payment_intent_id`. A
  decision belongs to an attempt, because the same intent can be attempted more
  than once and each attempt earns its own decision.

## 3. Fraud Detection Concepts Learned

- A relationship anchor is an *observation*, not a fact about a person. That is
  why `uk_payment_investigation_relation_alias` keys on `payment_attempt_id`:
  each attempt independently observed the identity, and collapsing those into
  one row would throw away when the identity was seen. The deduplication promise
  an analyst actually depends on — "show me this sibling once, with one reason
  list" — lives in the read path, not in the table.
- Coverage honesty is a fraud-decision concern, not a UI nicety. "No related
  payments" and "we could not check for related payments" lead to opposite
  decisions. Presenting a partial pivot as complete is how a ring gets approved.
- The most damaging possible forgery in this subsystem is not a malformed row.
  It is a *well-formed* anchor pointing at a real payment method with someone
  else's digest, because it fabricates a relationship that would survive analyst
  review. That is why the source-lineage trigger exists.

## 4. Security Concepts Learned

- Domain separation in an HMAC is what stops `hmac("customer.email", "x@y")`
  from colliding with `hmac("customer.emailx", "@y")`. Proving the boundary
  cannot be shifted is what actually establishes the NUL delimiter; observing
  one example does not.
- A fixed-width lowercase hex digest leaks nothing about input length. Asserting
  `[0-9a-f]{64}` on every stored digest is a real privacy claim, not a format
  check.
- Metrics are a privacy surface. They are routinely shipped to third-party
  backends with weaker access control than the database, so an unbounded tag
  value is simultaneously a cardinality incident and a data leak.
- Tenant isolation must be proven by byte-comparison of response bodies, not by
  status code. A foreign payment and an absent payment must be
  indistinguishable, and the only fields allowed to differ are the per-request
  identifier and timestamp.

## 5. Architecture / Design Notes

- Each navigation route declares a different guarantee because each answers a
  different question: the timeline declares `maximumStreamVersion` and
  `ordering`, webhook activity declares `WEBHOOK_TRANSPORT_EVIDENCE`, and
  related payments declares `SAME_MERCHANT_SERVER_DERIVED` and an `asOf`. A
  response that declares a consistency boundary must actually enforce it.
- The three routes do *not* name their row collection identically — `entries`
  versus `data`. That is a genuine published-contract wart, and the honest move
  was to register it as `TD-0006` rather than have the test quietly tolerate
  either name without naming why.
- Reading evidence must never alter the evidence. The scope proof takes a
  database fingerprint over events, anchors, attempts, and ledger entries before
  and after navigating, because otherwise the act of investigating changes what
  is being investigated.

## 7. Bugs, Surprises, Or Tradeoffs

- **Two guards were discovered, not two bugs.** `PaymentTimelineService.map`
  decodes the signed `event_record_bytes` and cross-checks the row's `id`,
  `event_version`, and `stream_id` against the values inside the signed record.
  A synthetic append built by replaying a real record under a fresh primary key
  was correctly refused with `503 timeline_integrity_unavailable`. Then the
  forged row could not even be deleted: `domain_events` UPDATE and DELETE raise
  `domain event history is immutable`. Both guards were promoted to their own
  tests, and the snapshot-stability claim was rebuilt on a genuine cancel
  append.
- **Two of my own claims were wrong, not the code.** I asserted anchors are
  unique per identity; they are correctly unique per attempt observation. I
  wrote a scope scan that would have banned the demo's guarded read-only
  reconciliation, which is a legitimate independent check. Both were corrected
  against real behavior rather than forced onto the system.
- Timestamps come back as `OffsetDateTime` under the PostgreSQL driver and
  `java.sql.Timestamp` under H2. Never cast; convert type-tolerantly.
- `MeterRegistry` is shared across test methods in one Spring context, so
  `isZero()` assertions are meaningless. Assert before-and-after deltas.
