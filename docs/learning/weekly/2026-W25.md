# Weekly Architecture Review: 2026-W25

## 1. Scope Reviewed

- Dates: 2026-06-19.
- Features: Chapter 10 confirm orchestration and Chapter 11 processor
  simulator authorization.
- Important diffs: confirm decision interface, processor-ready attempt state,
  authorization table, processor adapter, deterministic simulator catalog,
  README and learning trail updates.

## 2. What Changed Architecturally

- Confirm now acts as the first real-time orchestration bridge.
- Successful confirm moves PaymentIntent through `PROCESSING` and PaymentAttempt
  through `READY_FOR_PROCESSOR`, then calls the simulator.
- Processor results are now durable `Authorization` rows linked to
  PaymentAttempt.
- Approved results move PaymentIntent to `REQUIRES_CAPTURE`, retryable declines
  move it to `REQUIRES_PAYMENT_METHOD`, and 3DS-required results move it to
  `REQUIRES_ACTION`.
- Fraud decisioning is represented as an interface boundary, but durable
  `FraudDecision` and `FeatureSnapshot` remain Chapter 12 work.

## 3. Risks That Increased

- Payment lifecycle: capture eligibility now exists through `REQUIRES_CAPTURE`,
  so the next chapter that touches capture must prove ledger and capture
  invariants before money-movement simulation becomes richer.
- Fraud/ML lineage: the interface exists without durable lineage, so Chapter 12
  must not skip feature snapshot and freshness proof.

## 4. Risks That Decreased

- Confirm can no longer create unlimited successful attempts under separate
  idempotency keys after the first successful processing transition.
- Processor simulation now has a clear, durable attempt state to consume.
- Processor outcomes now have durable source-of-truth rows instead of only a
  response string.
- Retryable declines can create a second attempt with a new idempotency key.
- The platform avoids storing misleading thin fraud records before the fraud
  schema is ready.

## 5. Complexity Review

- Added a small decision-engine boundary, processor adapter, authorization
  domain, and payment state transitions.
- The complexity is justified because capture, refunds, disputes, analyst
  timelines, and fraud labels need durable processor truth.
- The stub must stay in-memory until durable fraud lineage is implemented.

## 6. Test Review

- Strongest tests added: confirm success proves approved authorization,
  deterministic declines, verification failures, 3DS-required behavior,
  idempotent replay, retry after decline, changed-body conflict, concurrent
  one-winner behavior, non-`ALLOW` fraud decisions never calling the processor,
  processor failure rollback from `PROCESSING`, unknown simulator card default
  approval, and no duplicate authorizations or fraud decisions.
- Missing tests a Staff engineer would ask for next: Chapter 12 durable
  FraudDecision and FeatureSnapshot coverage for approved and declined attempts.
- Tests that are too happy-path: none in the Chapter 10 confirm contract after
  the conflict and concurrency cases.

## 7. Deferrals

- Deferral: durable fraud lineage and feature freshness.
- Why it is acceptable: the current fraud table is too thin for product truth.
- Target day/phase: Chapter 12.
- Trigger to revisit: adding `FraudDecision` and `FeatureSnapshot` persistence.

- Deferral: capture, ledger, refund, and void side effects.
- Why it is acceptable: Chapter 11 proves authorization only; settlement-like
  side effects require double-entry ledger proof.
- Target day/phase: Phase 2 capture/ledger chapters.
- Trigger to revisit: implementing capture from `REQUIRES_CAPTURE`.

## 8. Rubric Update

- Strongest score: payment lifecycle reasoning.
- Weakest score: fraud lineage, still deferred until Chapter 12.
- Evidence: Chapter 10 and Chapter 11 transition, authorization, and contract
  tests.
- Next action: implement Chapter 12 durable FraudDecision and FeatureSnapshot.

## 9. Spaced Repetition Update

- Concepts added: confirm orchestration boundary, processor-ready attempt,
  processor authorization.
- Concepts reviewed: idempotency, tenant-scoped confirm, simulator card
  catalog, fraud lineage deferral.
- Concepts that still feel weak: feature snapshots and freshness proof.

## 10. Design Review Narrative

- This week we built the first real-time confirm orchestration bridge and the
  first durable processor authorization path.
- The main invariant was that a successful confirm creates one durable attempt
  and at most one durable authorization, while blocked/reviewed fraud decisions
  never cross the processor boundary.
- The production risk reduced was duplicate attempt creation, missing processor
  truth, stranded `PROCESSING` payments, and misleading fraud lineage.
- Review follow-up corrected migration discipline by moving authorizations into
  Flyway `V2`, and corrected money truth so non-approved outcomes authorize
  `0`.
- The tradeoff was adding processor truth without yet adding capture, ledger, or
  fraud lineage.
- The remaining risk is Chapter 12 fraud lineage completeness.
- Next, we should implement durable FraudDecision and FeatureSnapshot.
