# Daily Deep Dive: 2026-06-19

This note explains the Chapter 10 confirm-orchestration implementation.

## 1. System Goal

- Define confirm as the first real-time orchestration bridge.
- Move successful confirms to explicit processor-ready state.
- Avoid writing misleading thin fraud lineage before Chapter 12 owns the full
  fraud decision and feature snapshot contract.

## 2. Plain-English Design Brief

Confirm should not jump directly from object creation to fake fraud or fake
processor results.

Chapter 10 creates the shape of the real-time path:

- validate tenant-owned PaymentIntent and PaymentMethod
- create a PaymentAttempt under idempotency
- ask a decision-engine interface for a deterministic `ALLOW`
- move the attempt to `READY_FOR_PROCESSOR`
- move the PaymentIntent to `PROCESSING`

Chapter 10 intentionally does not persist `FraudDecision`, `FeatureSnapshot`,
processor authorization, or ledger entries.

## 3. Code Map

- `docs/IMPLEMENTATION_MASTER_PLAN.md` owns the chapter contract.
- `PaymentIntentController` owns confirm orchestration for this slice.
- `ConfirmDecisionEngine` owns the decisioning boundary.
- `StubConfirmDecisionEngine` returns `ALLOW` with `RULES_NOT_ENABLED`.
- `PaymentAttemptStatus` now includes `READY_FOR_PROCESSOR`.
- `PaymentAttempt.markReadyForProcessor()` owns the attempt transition guard.
- `PaymentIntent.markProcessingAfterConfirm()` owns the intent transition
  guard.
- `fraud_decisions` currently exists as a placeholder table; Chapter 12 should
  replace thin usage with durable fraud lineage.

## 4. Request/Data Flow

Implemented Chapter 10 flow:

1. Merchant calls `POST /payment_intents/{id}/confirm`.
2. `MerchantContext` and `Idempotency-Key` gate the mutation.
3. PaymentIntent is loaded through `(id, merchant_id)` with row lock.
4. PaymentMethod is loaded through `(id, merchant_id)` and checked against the
   PaymentIntent customer.
5. PaymentAttempt is created or replayed through idempotency.
6. The decision-engine interface returns in-memory `ALLOW`.
7. PaymentAttempt moves to `READY_FOR_PROCESSOR`.
8. PaymentIntent moves to `PROCESSING`.
9. The response remains the public PaymentAttempt response.

## 5. Database Invariants

- PaymentAttempt remains tenant-scoped and uniquely numbered per PaymentIntent.
- Idempotency remains unique by `(merchant_id, idempotency_key)`.
- Chapter 10 should not write `fraud_decisions` because the table does not yet
  contain the lineage fields required by the product vision.
- A successful Chapter 10 confirm leaves `payment_attempts.fraud_decision_id`,
  `payment_attempts.processor_authorization_id`, and
  `payment_attempts.three_ds_status` null.

## 6. Security And Privacy Invariants

- Confirm must keep using tenant-scoped lookups for both PaymentIntent and
  PaymentMethod.
- Logs should include request correlation and operation names, not sensitive
  card, key, idempotency hash, or merchant-secret material.

## 7. Fraud/ML Relevance

- The decision-engine interface gives Chapter 12 a clean insertion point for
  rules, feature snapshots, model timeout handling, and freshness metadata.
- Avoiding thin persisted fraud records protects future offline ML and analyst
  explanations from incomplete lineage.

## 9. Staff Engineer Review Notes

- Tradeoff: add an interface-level fraud decision stub now, but keep durable
  fraud lineage for Chapter 12.
- Risk reduced: prevent misleading fraud data from becoming product truth.
- Reversible choice: the in-memory `ALLOW` stub can be replaced by real rules
  without changing the confirm API shape.
- Dangerous overbuild: persisting thin `FraudDecision` records without score,
  reason codes, feature snapshot, latency, or freshness proof.

## 13. Interview Narrative

- The tradeoff I made was to introduce fraud decisioning as an interface
  boundary without persisting incomplete fraud lineage.
- The invariant I enforced was that confirm creates an auditable attempt and
  moves it only as far as processor-ready.
- The production risk I reduced was misleading fraud history polluting analyst
  views and future ML datasets.
- I deferred durable fraud decisions and feature snapshots because Chapter 12
  owns score, reason codes, latency, and freshness proof.
- The tests must prove confirm is idempotent, tenant-safe, stateful, and free
  of premature fraud, processor, and ledger side effects.
