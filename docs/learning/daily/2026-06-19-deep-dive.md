# Daily Deep Dive: 2026-06-19

This note explains the Chapter 10 confirm-orchestration implementation and the
Chapter 11 processor-simulator implementation.

## 1. System Goal

- Define confirm as the first real-time orchestration bridge.
- Move successful confirms to explicit processor-ready state.
- Avoid writing misleading thin fraud lineage before Chapter 12 owns the full
  fraud decision and feature snapshot contract.
- Turn the processor-ready boundary into durable authorization, decline, or
  action-required processor truth.

## 2. Plain-English Design Brief

Confirm should not jump directly from object creation to fake fraud or fake
processor results.

Chapter 10 creates the shape of the real-time path:

- validate tenant-owned PaymentIntent and PaymentMethod
- create a PaymentAttempt under idempotency
- ask a decision-engine interface for a deterministic `ALLOW`
- move the attempt to `READY_FOR_PROCESSOR`
- move the PaymentIntent to `PROCESSING`

Chapter 10 intentionally does not persist `FraudDecision`, `FeatureSnapshot`,
processor authorization, or ledger entries.

Chapter 11 extends this path by calling a deterministic `ProcessorAdapter`,
persisting a durable `Authorization`, mapping approved results to capture
eligibility, mapping retryable declines back to a confirmable payment state,
and mapping 3DS-required results to action-required states.

## 3. Code Map

- `docs/IMPLEMENTATION_MASTER_PLAN.md` owns the chapter contract.
- `PaymentIntentController` owns confirm orchestration for this slice.
- `ConfirmDecisionEngine` owns the decisioning boundary.
- `StubConfirmDecisionEngine` returns `ALLOW` with `RULES_NOT_ENABLED`.
- `PaymentAttemptStatus` now includes `READY_FOR_PROCESSOR`.
- `PaymentAttempt.markReadyForProcessor()` owns the attempt transition guard.
- `PaymentIntent.markProcessingAfterConfirm()` owns the intent transition
  guard.
- `fraud_decisions` currently exists as a placeholder table; Chapter 12 should
  replace thin usage with durable fraud lineage.
- `Authorization` stores full processor result truth.
- `ProcessorAdapter` hides simulator-specific logic from confirm orchestration.
- `SimulatorProcessorAdapter` maps safe card metadata to deterministic
  outcomes.

## 4. Request/Data Flow

Implemented Chapter 10 flow:

1. Merchant calls `POST /payment_intents/{id}/confirm`.
2. `MerchantContext` and `Idempotency-Key` gate the mutation.
3. PaymentIntent is loaded through `(id, merchant_id)` with row lock.
4. PaymentMethod is loaded through `(id, merchant_id)` and checked against the
   PaymentIntent customer.
5. PaymentAttempt is created or replayed through idempotency.
6. The decision-engine interface returns in-memory `ALLOW`.
7. PaymentAttempt moves to `READY_FOR_PROCESSOR`.
8. PaymentIntent moves to `PROCESSING`.
9. Chapter 11 calls the processor adapter.
10. Chapter 11 persists an Authorization record.
11. Chapter 11 maps the processor result:
   - approved -> PaymentAttempt `AUTHORIZED`, PaymentIntent `REQUIRES_CAPTURE`
   - decline -> PaymentAttempt `DECLINED`, PaymentIntent
     `REQUIRES_PAYMENT_METHOD`
   - 3DS required -> PaymentAttempt `REQUIRES_ACTION`, PaymentIntent
     `REQUIRES_ACTION`
12. The response remains the public PaymentAttempt response.

The whole confirm mutation runs inside the `IdempotencyService`
`TransactionTemplate`, so a processor exception rolls back the PaymentAttempt,
Authorization, PaymentIntent state mutation, and idempotency completion
together.

## 5. Database Invariants

- PaymentAttempt remains tenant-scoped and uniquely numbered per PaymentIntent.
- Idempotency remains unique by `(merchant_id, idempotency_key)`.
- Chapter 10 should not write `fraud_decisions` because the table does not yet
  contain the lineage fields required by the product vision.
- A successful Chapter 10 confirm leaves `payment_attempts.fraud_decision_id`,
  `payment_attempts.processor_authorization_id`, and
  `payment_attempts.three_ds_status` null.
- Chapter 11 writes one `authorizations` row per processor attempt.
- `authorizations` has a unique `(merchant_id, payment_attempt_id)` constraint
  so replay cannot create duplicate processor truth for the same attempt.
- `payment_attempts.processor_authorization_id` stores the simulator processor
  reference, but the authorization row remains the full source of truth.
- `authorizations.requested_amount` and `authorizations.authorized_amount`
  are separate facts so later partial authorization and capture logic can
  reason from durable truth.
- Approved authorizations set `authorized_amount` to the requested amount;
  declined and 3DS-required authorizations set it to `0`.
- `authorizations.three_ds_result` is non-null because every processor result
  should say whether 3DS was required, passed, failed, or not required.
- Chapter 11 authorizations are added through Flyway `V2` so `V1` remains
  immutable for environments that already applied it.

## 6. Security And Privacy Invariants

- Confirm must keep using tenant-scoped lookups for both PaymentIntent and
  PaymentMethod.
- Logs should include request correlation and operation names, not sensitive
  card, key, idempotency hash, or merchant-secret material.
- The processor simulator must not require raw PAN after PaymentMethod
  creation. Chapter 11 uses safe stored metadata: `card_bin` and `card_last4`.
- The simulator catalog key is `card_bin:card_last4`. That keeps the lookup
  explicit and reviewable instead of hiding a fragile string concatenation.

## 7. Fraud/ML Relevance

- The decision-engine interface gives Chapter 12 a clean insertion point for
  rules, feature snapshots, model timeout handling, and freshness metadata.
- Avoiding thin persisted fraud records protects future offline ML and analyst
  explanations from incomplete lineage.
- Processor declines and verification failures are future fraud/card-testing
  signals. They are persisted as processor truth first, then Chapter 12 can
  attach feature snapshots and fraud decisions without inventing history.

## 9. Staff Engineer Review Notes

- Tradeoff: add an interface-level fraud decision stub now, but keep durable
  fraud lineage for Chapter 12.
- Risk reduced: prevent misleading fraud data from becoming product truth.
- Risk reduced: avoid processor/network side effects for payments that a future
  fraud engine marks `BLOCK` or `REVIEW`.
- Risk reduced: avoid stranded PaymentIntents by proving processor exceptions
  roll back the transaction boundary.
- Review choice: do not add a decorative `@Transactional` annotation to a
  self-invoked controller helper. The existing idempotency transaction template
  is the effective boundary.
- Reversible choice: the in-memory `ALLOW` stub can be replaced by real rules
  without changing the confirm API shape.
- Dangerous overbuild: persisting thin `FraudDecision` records without score,
  reason codes, feature snapshot, latency, or freshness proof.
- Chapter 11 tradeoff: store processor authorization as its own table instead
  of relying on `payment_attempts.processor_authorization_id`.
- Risk reduced: later capture, refunds, disputes, fraud labels, and analyst
  timelines can depend on full processor truth.
- Reversible choice: the simulator is behind `ProcessorAdapter`, so a future
  real PSP adapter can replace it without changing confirm orchestration.
- Dangerous shortcut avoided: using raw PAN downstream of PaymentMethod
  tokenization.

## 13. Interview Narrative

- The tradeoff I made was to introduce fraud decisioning as an interface
  boundary without persisting incomplete fraud lineage.
- The invariant I enforced was that confirm creates an auditable attempt and
  moves it only as far as processor-ready.
- The production risk I reduced was misleading fraud history polluting analyst
  views and future ML datasets.
- I deferred durable fraud decisions and feature snapshots because Chapter 12
  owns score, reason codes, latency, and freshness proof.
- The tests must prove confirm is idempotent, tenant-safe, stateful, and free
  of premature fraud, processor, and ledger side effects.
- The Chapter 11 tradeoff I made was to persist full processor authorization
  records while keeping capture and ledger writes deferred.
- The Chapter 11 invariant I enforced was one durable authorization per
  processor attempt, with idempotent replay returning the stored response.
- The production risk I reduced was losing processor response truth needed for
  disputes, fraud labels, card-testing analysis, and capture eligibility.
- The tests prove approved, declined, verification failure, and 3DS-required
  paths without creating fraud lineage or duplicate authorizations.
