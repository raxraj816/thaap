# Daily Deep Dive: 2026-08-03

Chapter 54 — Canonical Timeline And Safe Related-Payment Pivots.

## 1. System Goal

- We built the two screens an analyst reads seconds before deciding whether real
  money moves: the exact history of one payment, and the list of other payments
  that look like they came from the same person.
- In a payments/fraud platform these are the difference between a decision and a
  guess. A fraud analyst who cannot see what actually happened, in order, cannot
  explain a block to a merchant or defend it to a regulator. An analyst who
  cannot pivot from one bad payment to its siblings blocks one card while the
  ring keeps charging on nine others.
- The production risk addressed is *silent wrongness*. A timeline that reorders
  itself, drops an event under pagination, or renders an unmapped event as a
  blank row does not throw an error. It quietly produces a confident, wrong
  story. Same for a pivot that misses a sibling, invents one, or crosses a
  tenant boundary.

## 2. Plain-English Design Brief

- **Mental model.** A payment has an append-only stream of facts, each with a
  version number `1, 2, 3, ...`. The timeline is that stream, translated into
  safe human-readable entries through a closed lookup table, and handed out in
  pages. Separately, each payment attempt writes down keyed digests of the
  identities it saw — this email, this phone, this card. Two payments are
  "related" when they wrote down the same digest. Nobody ever sees the digest.
- **Without this design.** If the timeline sorted by business timestamp, two
  events in the same microsecond could swap and a decision would appear to
  precede its cause. If the mapper were open rather than closed, a new event
  type would render whatever happened to be in its payload, including note text,
  webhook URLs, and raw metadata. If relationships were computed by comparing
  raw emails at read time, the raw email would have to be in the query, in the
  logs, and one join away from another tenant's rows.
- **Built now.** Reading, paging, presenting, relating, and the durable
  constraints that keep an anchor honest.
- **Deferred.** Cross-merchant graph intelligence (Phase 8), saved views and
  bulk assignment (Chapter 55), insights (Chapter 56), and envelope-name
  unification (`TD-0006`, Chapter 59).

## 3. Code Map

- `PaymentTimelineService` — reads the canonical stream by tenant, payment, and
  version; decodes and verifies each signed record; maps each type through the
  closed presentation table; owns `TimelinePage` and `WebhookActivityPage`.
- `RelatedPaymentService` — resolves the seed payment's identities, finds
  sibling anchors within the merchant and the window, and owns
  `RelatedPaymentPage` and its `Coverage`.
- `InvestigationRelationAnchorWriter` — writes one anchor per attempt per
  identity, guarded by a per-attempt existence check.
- `CorrelationValueHasher` — the keyed, domain-separated digest.
- `WorkspaceCursorCodec` — signs and binds cursors to a reader, a payment, and a
  view domain.
- `DecisionIntelligenceMetrics` — the bounded meter vocabulary.
- V86 creates `payment_investigation_relation_anchors`; V87 adds the source
  lineage and append-only triggers.
- `Phase6Workspace.tsx` renders both sections independently.

## 4. Request/Data Flow

1. `GET /risk/workspace/payments/{id}/timeline` arrives with an operator
   credential header.
2. The operator is resolved live and the payment is resolved *tenant-first*, so
   a foreign payment is indistinguishable from an absent one. `limit` is
   range-checked; a `cursor`, if present, must verify against the reader, this
   payment, and the timeline domain before any anchor inside it is even parsed.
3. Nothing is mutated. This is a pure read, and the scope proof takes a database
   fingerprint before and after to prove it.
4. Each row's signed `event_record_bytes` is decoded and its embedded `id`,
   `event_version`, and `stream_id` are cross-checked against the database row.
   A disagreement is a `503`, not a rendered row.
5. Each event is mapped through the closed presentation table. An unknown field,
   an unsupported version, or a structured value fails closed. The page declares
   `maximumStreamVersion`, and every later page holds that ceiling.

## 5. Database Invariants

- `domain_events` versions per stream are exactly `1..n`: no gap, no duplicate,
  enforced by `ck_domain_events_stream_version` and
  `uk_domain_events_stream_version`. History cannot be updated or deleted at
  all — the engine raises `domain event history is immutable`. Without this, a
  bad actor or a well-meaning operator could "repair" the record of why money
  was blocked.
- Every anchor's digest must match its named source row, enforced by a deferred
  constraint trigger. Without it, a well-formed forged anchor would fabricate a
  fraud ring that survived review.
- Anchors are append-only, drawn from a closed relation vocabulary, paired to a
  compatible source kind, unique per `(merchant, attempt, type, digest, key
  version)`, and their digests must be trimmed and of plausible length. The
  trimming rule matters more than it looks: a padded digest compares unequal to
  the same identity written unpadded, silently splitting one person into two.

## 6. Security And Privacy Invariants

- The sensitive values are the customer's email, phone, card, and the operator's
  credential.
- None are stored raw on the investigation path. Each becomes a keyed,
  domain-separated HMAC — deliberately *not* an unkeyed SHA-256, which would be
  trivially rainbow-tabled for a phone number. The digest is fixed-width lowercase
  hex, so it leaks nothing about input length.
- The digest never leaves the database. The API publishes only a reason code
  such as `SAME_EMAIL`. The browser never receives it, and no meter carries it.
- The operator credential lives only in process memory: never storage, cookies,
  the URL, or the DOM.
- The principle is that correlation should be *usable* without being
  *disclosable*. An analyst needs to know two payments share a phone; nobody
  needs to see the phone.

## 7. Fraud/ML Relevance

- Anchors are the raw material for future velocity features, ring detection, and
  network intelligence, and because each anchor records *when it was observed*
  by *which attempt*, they can be replayed as of any historical moment. That is
  what makes a future feature trainable without leakage.
- We deliberately did not turn a relationship into a score, a feature, or an
  authorization input. Source scans prove neither risk decisioning nor
  authorization reads the anchor table. The moment an unlabelled correlation
  becomes silent policy, it stops being reviewable, and a fraud platform whose
  decisions cannot be explained is a liability.

## 8. Tests As Proof

- Order and completeness: `PaymentTimelineContractTests`, reconciled against
  `domain_events`.
- Fail-closed presentation: `PaymentTimelinePrivacyTests`, exhaustive over the
  event enum.
- Page stability under a real concurrent append, plus history immutability and a
  version race: `PaymentTimelineAppendPostgreSqlTests`.
- Cursor unforgeability, proven by mutating every character:
  `PaymentTimelineCursorTests`.
- Durable constraints, each exercised individually on PostgreSQL:
  `PaymentRelationshipMigrationPostgreSqlTests`.
- Tenant indistinguishability by scrubbed byte comparison:
  `PaymentRelationshipTenantIsolationTests`.
- The weakest area is still *scale*. Every proof runs on a handful of payments.
  The test a Staff engineer would ask for next is a saturation proof: a seed
  identity with thousands of siblings, asserting the pivot stays bounded in rows
  read and time taken, and that the coverage callout degrades honestly rather
  than truncating silently.

## 9. Staff Engineer Review Notes

- **Tradeoff.** We kept one anchor row per attempt rather than collapsing to one
  per identity. That costs rows and makes the naive uniqueness assertion wrong,
  but it preserves *when* each identity was seen, which is what makes historical
  replay and future feature training possible. Deduplication moved to the read
  path where the product promise actually lives.
- **Risk reduced.** Silent wrongness. Order, completeness, page stability,
  fail-closed presentation, tenant indistinguishability, and anchor lineage are
  all now proven at the boundary that enforces them rather than assumed.
- **Kept reversible.** The envelope naming split was registered as `TD-0006`
  instead of being fixed in a hurry on a published route.
- **Dangerous to overbuild.** Graph traversal, scoring, or cross-merchant
  intelligence. Each would commit the product to a contract and a privacy
  posture nobody has reviewed, on top of anchors that are only a day old.

## 13. Interview Narrative

- The tradeoff I made was keeping one relationship anchor per payment attempt
  rather than one per identity, accepting row growth to preserve when each
  identity was observed, and moving deduplication to the read path where the
  analyst-facing promise actually lives.
- The invariant I enforced was that a payment's history is exact, ordered by
  stream version, stable under concurrent appends, fail-closed on anything
  unrecognised, and immutable once written.
- The production risk I reduced was silent wrongness: a confident, wrong story
  told to an analyst about to move money.
- I deferred envelope-name unification because renaming a field on a published
  route is a breaking change that must be decided once for the whole navigation
  surface; it is registered as `TD-0006` against the Chapter 59 contract freeze.
- The tests prove it at the boundary that enforces it: concurrency, immutability
  and constraint claims on real PostgreSQL with two connections, cursor
  unforgeability by mutating every character, tenant indistinguishability by
  byte-comparing scrubbed bodies, and privacy by canaries drawn from real rows
  and asserted present before being asserted absent.
