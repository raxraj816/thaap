# Weekly Architecture Review: 2026-W25

## 1. Scope Reviewed

- Dates: 2026-06-19.
- Features: Chapter 10 confirm orchestration.
- Important diffs: Chapter 10 plan update, confirm decision interface,
  processor-ready attempt state, README and learning trail updates.

## 2. What Changed Architecturally

- Confirm now acts as the first real-time orchestration bridge.
- Successful confirm moves PaymentIntent to `PROCESSING` and PaymentAttempt to
  `READY_FOR_PROCESSOR`.
- Fraud decisioning is represented as an interface boundary, but durable
  `FraudDecision` and `FeatureSnapshot` remain Chapter 12 work.

## 3. Risks That Increased

- Payment lifecycle: `PROCESSING` now exists in a real path, so Chapter 11 must
  own the next transition carefully.
- Fraud/ML lineage: the interface exists without durable lineage, so Chapter 12
  must not skip feature snapshot and freshness proof.

## 4. Risks That Decreased

- Confirm can no longer create unlimited successful attempts under separate
  idempotency keys after the first successful processing transition.
- Processor simulation now has a clear, durable attempt state to consume.
- The platform avoids storing misleading thin fraud records before the fraud
  schema is ready.

## 5. Complexity Review

- Added a small decision-engine boundary and two domain transition methods.
- The complexity is justified because Chapter 11 and Chapter 12 need stable
  insertion points.
- The stub must stay in-memory until durable fraud lineage is implemented.

## 6. Test Review

- Strongest tests added: confirm success proves `READY_FOR_PROCESSOR`,
  `PROCESSING`, idempotent replay, changed-body conflict, concurrent one-winner
  behavior, and no fraud/processor side effects.
- Missing tests a Staff engineer would ask for next: Chapter 11 processor
  outcome mapping, authorization persistence, and retryability tests.
- Tests that are too happy-path: none in the Chapter 10 confirm contract after
  the conflict and concurrency cases.

## 7. Deferrals

- Deferral: processor authorization.
- Why it is acceptable: Chapter 10 stops at a processor-ready attempt.
- Target day/phase: Chapter 11.
- Trigger to revisit: implementing deterministic card outcome mapping.

- Deferral: durable fraud lineage and feature freshness.
- Why it is acceptable: the current fraud table is too thin for product truth.
- Target day/phase: Chapter 12.
- Trigger to revisit: adding `FraudDecision` and `FeatureSnapshot` persistence.

## 8. Rubric Update

- Strongest score: payment lifecycle reasoning.
- Weakest score: processor authorization modeling, still deferred.
- Evidence: Chapter 10 transition and contract tests.
- Next action: design Chapter 11 authorization persistence.

## 9. Spaced Repetition Update

- Concepts added: confirm orchestration boundary, processor-ready attempt.
- Concepts reviewed: idempotency, tenant-scoped confirm, fraud lineage deferral.
- Concepts that still feel weak: deterministic simulator card catalog design.

## 10. Design Review Narrative

- This week we built the first real-time confirm orchestration bridge.
- The main invariant was that a successful confirm creates one durable attempt
  and moves it only as far as processor-ready.
- The production risk reduced was duplicate attempt creation and misleading
  fraud lineage.
- The tradeoff was adding a decision interface without persisting fraud records.
- The remaining risk is Chapter 11 simulator card catalog design and Chapter
  12 fraud lineage completeness.
- Next, we should implement deterministic processor authorization.
